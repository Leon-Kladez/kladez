# What to do if…

Short checklists for an emergency, so you do not have to search in a panic. **If you have a signal, call 112 first** (it works from any phone, with no credit and even without a SIM card). These notes do not replace a doctor or rescuers; for more, see the Medical Wikipedia in the [Library](/library) and ask the [AI assistant](/chat).

## Emergency numbers (Russia)

| Number | Service |
|---|---|
| **112** | Single emergency number: fire, ambulance, police, gas |
| 101 | Fire and rescue |
| 102 | Police |
| 103 | Ambulance |
| 104 | Gas emergency |

Speak calmly: **what happened, where (address or landmarks), how many are hurt, who is calling**. Do not hang up first.

## Someone is unconscious

1. Make sure you are not in danger (fire, electricity, traffic).
2. Call out loudly and shake the shoulders gently.
3. Check breathing: tilt the head back, lift the chin, and look, listen and feel for 10 seconds.
4. **Breathing**: roll them onto their side (head slightly back, upper leg bent), call for help and keep checking breathing.
5. **Not breathing**: call 112 and start **CPR**: hands in the centre of the chest, arms straight, press 5–6 cm deep at 100–120 times a minute. If you are trained, give 2 rescue breaths after every 30 compressions. Do not stop until help arrives or the person starts breathing.

## Heavy bleeding

1. Put on gloves or a plastic bag if you have them.
2. **Press hard on the wound** with clean cloth, a bandage or your hand, and keep pressing.
3. Put a tight bandage over it.
4. If blood spurts from an arm or leg or the bandage soaks through quickly, apply a **tourniquet** (belt, scarf) 5–7 cm above the wound over clothing and tighten until the bleeding stops. **Write down the time** and tuck the note under it. Keep it on no longer than 1 hour in winter or 2 hours in summer; get to a hospital before then.
5. Call 103 or 112.

## Burns

1. Get away from the fire and put out burning clothes (cover with thick cloth, roll on the ground).
2. **Cool under cool running water for 15–20 minutes.**
3. Do not pull off stuck clothing, do not pop blisters, **do not apply oil, cream or sour cream**.
4. Cover with a clean dry cloth. A large burn, or a burn to the face, hands or groin, or on a child, needs a doctor.

## Fire at home

1. Call 101 or 112.
2. Leave at once, without belongings. In smoke, stay low and cover your nose and mouth with a damp cloth.
3. Do not use the lift. Close doors behind you; it slows the fire.
4. If you cannot get out, shut yourself in a room, block gaps with wet rags, open the window and call for help; do not jump from a height.

## You smell gas

1. **Do not switch lights or appliances on or off**, no flames, do not phone from inside.
2. Turn off the gas and open the windows.
3. Leave and warn neighbours by knocking (not with the doorbell).
4. Call 104 or 112 from outside.

## Poisoning

1. Find out what and how much (keep the packaging).
2. Call 103.
3. **Do not make them vomit** if it was acid, alkali or petrol, or if they are drowsy.
4. If unconscious, put them on their side and watch their breathing.

## Power cut

- Keep torches and candles in one place; keep candles away from curtains and never leave them unattended.
- Do not open the fridge needlessly: food keeps about 4 hours in it and up to a day in the freezer.
- Unplug appliances so a surge does not damage them when power returns.
- Run generators and gas or charcoal heaters **outdoors only**: indoors, carbon monoxide kills silently.
- On a laptop, Kladez runs on battery: maps, reference books and the library work without internet.

## No water

- Store **3 litres per person per day** (drinking and cooking), ideally for 3 days.
- **Boil doubtful water for 1–3 minutes** after it comes to the boil. Let cloudy water settle and strain it through cloth first.
- If you cannot boil it, use water purification tablets as directed.

## “Attention everyone!” signal (siren)

Turn on the radio or TV: all local channels broadcast what happened and what to do. Follow the instructions. Ordinary FM radio works without internet, on batteries or in a car.

## Lost in the forest

1. Stop and do not rush about. If you have a signal, call 112 and describe what you see.
2. Listen for roads, trains, dogs or tractors and head towards the sound.
3. Follow a stream or river downstream, power lines or cleared tracks.
4. Before dark, prepare for the night: shelter from the wind, a bed of spruce branches, a fire.
5. Bright clothing and noise help rescuers find you.

## Communication without internet: radios and frequencies

When mobile networks are down, radios still work. In Russia you can use these without registration:

| Radios | Band | Channels | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PMR446** | 446.0 MHz | 8 (16 on newer sets) | The most common walkie-talkies, up to a few kilometres |
| **LPD** | 433 MHz | 69 | Low power, a few hundred metres in a city |
| **CB** | 27 MHz | 40 per grid | Car and base sets, up to tens of kilometres |

On CB, **channel 9 (27.065 MHz)** is the emergency channel and **channel 15 (27.135 MHz, AM)** is used by drivers and truckers, so someone is most often listening there.

Agree with your family in advance **which channel and at what time** to switch radios on if networks go down.

You may listen to service frequencies (aviation, maritime, emergency services) but must not transmit on them.
